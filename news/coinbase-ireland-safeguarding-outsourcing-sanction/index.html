Coinbase Ireland Accepts €3.25 Million Sanction Over Safeguarding and Outsourcing Failures

The absence of customer losses does not establish that safeguarding controls worked as required.

By The Legal CircleAnalysisOctober 22, 20262 min read
  • #Coinbase Ireland fine
Legal Developments

Analysis

Published by The Legal Circle

October 22, 2026

Ireland; Canadian comparison

The absence of customer losses does not establish that safeguarding controls worked as required.

Ireland’s central bank announced an enforcement settlement with Coinbase Ireland on October 22 covering safeguarding and outsourcing breaches between November 2020 and August 2025.

Coinbase Ireland admitted the contraventions and accepted a reprimand and monetary penalty of €3,252,618 after a settlement discount. The sanctions require High Court confirmation and take effect once confirmed. Central Bank of Ireland: enforcement settlement and High Court confirmation.

Safeguarding and outsourced oversight

The regulator identified excessive reliance on a US group company, insufficient local oversight and expertise, inadequate systems and testing, and prolonged underfunding of the designated safeguarding account. Central Bank of Ireland: enforcement settlement and High Court confirmation.

Coinbase says it self-reported the issues and that customers suffered neither financial loss nor inability to access funds as a result. Reuters: Coinbase response, via MarketScreener.

Those points address different questions. Customer outcomes matter, but preventative obligations are designed to protect against failures before losses occur.

Local management needs real capability

For regulated financial businesses, the outsourcing findings are particularly instructive. An arrangement within a corporate group still requires effective oversight by the regulated entity.

Counsel should examine whether local management can understand the outsourced function, obtain reliable information and respond when controls fail. A contract assigning tasks does not, by itself, demonstrate that those capabilities exist.

The action also highlights escalation. The regulator identified weaknesses in procedures for recognizing and reporting material safeguarding changes. Central Bank of Ireland: enforcement settlement and High Court confirmation.

Escalation and court confirmation

That makes incident governance part of the control environment: who identifies the issue, who receives it and who decides what must be reported?

For Canadian practitioners, the Irish requirements remain jurisdiction-specific. The broader governance lesson is to assess the regulated entity’s actual capacity, including where a better-resourced affiliate performs the work.

The pending High Court step should remain visible in reporting about the sanction’s status.

Outsourcing can move a function. It does not remove the need for effective oversight.
