Tax Court of Canada Proposes Email Service, Redaction and Longer Motion Deadlines

Three routine tasks in a Tax Court appeal could change under proposed rules: serving documents by email, checking filings for private information and counting back from a motion hearing.

Canada

Consultation - proposed rules

Three routine tasks in a Tax Court appeal could change under proposed rules: serving documents by email, checking filings for private information and counting back from a motion hearing.

The Tax Court of Canada Rules Committee’s text is dated October 26, 2026. It appeared in the November 14 edition of the Canada Gazette, Part I. The rules are proposed, subject to Governor in Council approval, and open for representations for 60 days after publication.

Issue What the proposal would change
Email service Permit it in specified circumstances, including consent in an originating document or later consent. Listing an email address in a reply would also be a specified basis.
Sensitive information Require parties to omit or redact specified information from pleadings and documents, including social insurance numbers. Some other categories have exceptions where the information is relevant to the proceeding.
Motions on notice under rules 67 and 68 Change the lead time for serving and filing relevant documents from 7 to 21 days for the moving party and from 2 to 14 days for the responding party.

The proposal also addresses service outside Canada and would put some existing discovery practices into the rules. The package’s regulatory analysis presents the longer motion deadlines as a way to give parties and the Court more time to review materials before a hearing.

For a litigation team, these changes would affect different points in a file. A service checklist would need to record the basis for using email. A filing checklist would need a separate privacy review. A motion calendar would need to leave considerably more time for both sides to assemble and serve their materials.

Those are changes counsel can evaluate during consultation. The existing rules and any applicable court directions still govern active files.

Sources: Canada Gazette proposed rules and regulatory analysis
