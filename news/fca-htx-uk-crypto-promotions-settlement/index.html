FCA Announces HTX Settlement and Court Undertakings on UK Crypto Promotions

Britain’s Financial Conduct Authority has announced a settlement with Huobi Global S.A., associated with crypto exchange HTX, that includes undertakings to the High Court concerning unlawful financial promotions.

Analysis

October 29, 2026

United Kingdom; Canadian financial-regulation comparison

Britain’s Financial Conduct Authority has announced a settlement with Huobi Global S.A., associated with crypto exchange HTX, that includes undertakings to the High Court concerning unlawful financial promotions.

The FCA announced the outcome on October 29. The consent order is dated October 27 and records a confidential settlement agreement dated October 16.

The settlement does not constitute an admission of liability or wrongdoing.

What the undertakings cover

Huobi Global undertakes not to knowingly communicate, directly or indirectly, financial promotions contrary to section 21 of the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000.

The order expressly covers the HTX website, software applications and social-media channels. The undertakings run until October 21, 2031.

The proceedings are stayed on the settlement terms, except for enforcement.

What the FCA says has changed

The regulator says HTX is no longer active in the UK: new UK users cannot register, consumers cannot make deposits, and the platform will not actively target UK consumers.

The FCA began the proceedings in October 2025. Its announcement directs existing users to HTX’s information about residual assets and withdrawal arrangements.

Why the outcome matters

For counsel advising overseas platforms, the case illustrates how a domestic financial-promotion regime can affect communications delivered through global websites, applications and social channels.

The precise legal outcome matters. This is a settlement supported by court undertakings, not a judgment establishing all the regulator’s original allegations.

That distinction preserves both sides of the story: the restrictions are concrete and enforceable, while liability has not been admitted.
