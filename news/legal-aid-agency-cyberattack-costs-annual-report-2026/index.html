Legal Aid Agency Reports £18.8 Million in Cyber-Incident Spending

Analysis

Published by The Legal Circle

November 18, 2026

England and Wales

Annual report - April 2025 to March 2026

The cost of a cyberattack on a justice institution extends beyond restoring its systems. It also includes keeping applications moving and ensuring providers can continue working while normal processes are unavailable.

The Legal Aid Agency’s annual performance report, published November 17, 2026, records £18.8 million in operational and digital spending on its cyber incident during the 2025-26 financial year. This is new reporting about an earlier attack - not the announcement of a new breach.

The agency says evidence indicates that compromise began in late December 2024. It identified suspicious activity in April 2025. Contingency measures included accepting urgent civil applications by email and maintaining provider payments through alternative arrangements.

An average-payment scheme distributed more than £394 million during the disruption. That sum represents payments supporting providers; it is not the reported cost of the attack.

The distinction matters when assessing the report. Incident-response expenditure measures one part of the financial impact. Payments that would otherwise have flowed through ordinary systems describe the scale of continuity arrangements, rather than an additional loss.

For Canadian legal organisations, the report offers a practical planning question: what happens to the work when the system used to administer it becomes unavailable? A credible continuity plan needs more than backups. It needs a way to identify urgent cases, communicate with providers, authorise decisions and reconcile payments afterwards.

Those processes also need testing. An organisation should know which manual steps preserve service and which introduce new risks, including duplicate payments, missing records or insecure exchanges of sensitive information.

A restored system is one recovery milestone. Keeping legal assistance available during the outage is another.

Sources: Legal Aid Agency 2025-26 performance report
