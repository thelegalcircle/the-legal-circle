Ontario Launches Single-Judge Case Management Pilot for Complex Criminal Cases

Complex criminal cases require sustained attention to disclosure, applications and scheduling. Ontario’s latest procedural pilot aims to provide more continuity in that work.

By The Legal CircleAnalysisOctober 13, 20264 min read
  • #Ontario single-judge case management
Legal Developments

Analysis

Published by The Legal Circle

October 13, 2026

Ontario, Canada

Complex criminal cases require sustained attention to disclosure, applications and scheduling. Ontario’s latest procedural pilot aims to provide more continuity in that work.

Effective October 13, the Ontario Court of Justice’s practice direction establishes a single-judge case-management stream for eligible complex matters. The same judicial officer is to manage the matter through its case-management phase.

The pilot operates in select locations across the province. Eligible categories include homicide, attempted murder, specified child sexual offences, human trafficking, criminal organization offences, and terrorism and other national-security offences. The initiative is a procedural pilot, rather than a change to substantive criminal law.

For Crown and defence counsel, the immediate significance is how an eligible matter moves through court and how early its procedural needs are identified.

How a case enters the stream

The direction provides that first appearances remain in regular case-management court. The presiding judicial officer identifies eligible complex matters for adjournment into the single-judge stream.

The assigned judge can manage scheduling, conduct judicial pretrials where appropriate and consider referral for a statutory case-management appointment or early assignment of a preliminary-inquiry judge.

The direction also contemplates setting timelines for materials and disclosure, confirming time estimates and scheduling applications. It distinguishes disclosure timelines in this stream from court orders.

That distinction matters when counsel describe what has been directed and what legal authority supports it.

Continuity does not mean one judge for every stage

The pilot’s promise is continuity during case management. It should not be described as a guarantee that the same judge will conduct every later stage of the proceeding.

Practitioners should distinguish the single-judge stream from any subsequent statutory appointment and identify the authority being exercised at each stage.

This is especially important where scheduling discussions, preliminary-inquiry arrangements and adjudication of applications intersect.

The potential benefit is accumulated knowledge

A recurring case-management judge can develop an understanding of the file’s procedural history, unresolved issues and previous commitments.

In practical terms, that could reduce time spent repeatedly explaining why a matter is not ready to proceed. It could also help expose dependencies: an application awaiting particular disclosure, a hearing estimate dependent on witness availability, or a scheduling issue affecting several connected matters.

Those are potential benefits, rather than established results. The pilot’s effectiveness will depend on implementation, resources and the quality of information counsel provide.

How counsel can prepare

A useful case-management presentation should make the remaining work visible.

Counsel can identify outstanding disclosure with specificity, explain anticipated applications, provide realistic estimates and distinguish confirmed scheduling commitments from assumptions.

A clear chronology can also show which steps have been completed and which require action from another participant.

Continuity increases the value of accurate updates. If the same judge returns to the file, inconsistent estimates and unexplained changes are more likely to require an account.

The pilot creates an opportunity for earlier, more sustained management of difficult proceedings. Whether it reduces delay will require evidence from its operation.

Judicial continuity can help a complex case move forward when counsel make the next steps clear.
