Ottawa Gives Cleveland-Cliffs 15 Days Over Stelco Employment Commitments

The Stelco employment dispute has moved to a formal deadline.

By The Legal CircleAnalysisOctober 23, 20262 min read
  • #Stelco employment undertakings
Legal Developments

Analysis

Published by The Legal Circle

October 23, 2026

Canada

The Stelco employment dispute has moved to a formal deadline.

Industry Minister Mélanie Joly has given Cleveland-Cliffs 15 days to address planned Ontario job cuts that Ottawa alleges breach acquisition commitments. The company denies a breach.

Reuters reports that the government warned of potential court proceedings seeking remedies including divestiture and daily penalties. The warning is not a court finding or an order requiring a sale. Reuters: deadline and company response, via MarketScreener.

A deadline, not a court order

The commitments originated in the Investment Canada Act review of Cleveland-Cliffs’ acquisition of Stelco.

Ottawa’s October 2024 approval statement publicly identified binding five-year undertakings, including maintaining the number of unionized employees and the vast majority of non-union employees. Government of Canada: acquisition undertakings.

The current dispute concerns how those commitments apply after closing.

Post-closing compliance needs ownership

For foreign-investment counsel, the development illustrates why undertakings require continuing governance. Responsibility cannot end when the acquisition team closes the transaction.

A business should know who monitors compliance, which operational decisions require legal review and how concerns reach the people responsible for communications with government.

The wording of the undertaking remains central. A public description helps explain the commitment but cannot resolve every interpretive question about its conditions, duration or application.

Watch the response and any proceedings

For the same reason, changed commercial circumstances require a legal assessment rather than an assumption that the original promise has ceased to apply.

The enforcement warning also requires precision. The possibility of a court-ordered remedy is materially different from a minister having already imposed it.

The next reporting question is what the company does within the stated period and whether Ottawa commences proceedings.

Canadian workers, investors and counterparties have substantial interests in the outcome. For lawyers, the immediate issue remains the content and enforceability of the undertaking.

Acquisition approval can create obligations that remain consequential long after the transaction closes.
