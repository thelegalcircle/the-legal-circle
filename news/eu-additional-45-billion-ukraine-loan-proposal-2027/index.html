EU Proposes Additional €45 Billion Ukraine Loan for 2027

Analysis

Published by The Legal Circle

November 18, 2026

European Union

Legislative proposals - awaiting approval

The European Commission has proposed additional financing for Ukraine in 2027, combining new EU lending with changes to how future proceeds from immobilised Russian assets could support repayment.

Announced on November 18, 2026, the package proposes up to €45 billion in additional support. It remains subject to EU legislative approval. The announcement does not mean the new loan has been enacted, contracted or disbursed.

The proposed financing would involve EU borrowing backed by the EU budget. Related amendments would allow future windfall profits from immobilised Russian assets to contribute to repayment of eligible loans. The Commission describes a structure under which Ukraine would be required to repay principal once it receives reparations from Russia.

The proposal is additional to the existing €90 billion Ukraine Support Loan. The remaining 2027 allocation under that earlier instrument is a separate source of support and should not be confused with the new proposal.

For lawyers following sanctions and sovereign financing, several legal steps need to remain distinct: authority to borrow, the budget guarantee, conditions for disbursement and the permitted use of asset-generated proceeds. Approval of one element does not automatically establish the others.

The same care applies to terminology. Using profits generated by immobilised assets is different from transferring ownership of the underlying assets. A financing announcement should not be described as a completed confiscation.

As the legislative texts advance, the useful questions will concern repayment triggers, the sequencing of existing obligations and who bears the risk if expected proceeds are delayed or insufficient.

The proposed amount attracts attention. The allocation of repayment risk will determine how the arrangement works.
