US Supreme Court Leaves Transgender Prisoner Injunction in Place While Litigation Continues

The US Supreme Court has declined to suspend an injunction protecting access to gender-affirming care for transgender federal prisoners while litigation proceeds.

By The Legal CircleAnalysisOctober 22, 20262 min read
  • #Trump v Kingdom Supreme Court

Analysis

Published by The Legal Circle

October 22, 2026

United States; Canadian comparison

The US Supreme Court has declined to suspend an injunction protecting access to gender-affirming care for transgender federal prisoners while litigation proceeds.

Its October 21 order in Trump v. Kingdom, No. 26A416, denied the administration’s stay application without substantive reasons. Justices Clarence Thomas and Samuel Alito would have granted it. US Supreme Court: Trump v. Kingdom docket.

The immediate effect is to leave the lower-court injunction against the challenged Bureau of Prisons restrictions in place. The Supreme Court has not issued a final ruling on the policy’s legality. US Supreme Court: Trump v. Kingdom docket.

An emergency order, not a merits ruling

That procedural distinction matters particularly where the practical consequences are substantial.

An emergency application asks whether a lower-court order should operate during further proceedings. Its disposition does not necessarily resolve the underlying statutory or constitutional claims.

Here, the absence of reasons also limits what can responsibly be inferred. The order does not identify which considerations persuaded the justices or establish a detailed test for future medical-treatment disputes.

What can be inferred from a stay denial

For Canadian lawyers following comparative prison litigation, the useful questions concern procedural posture, the exact protection supplied by the injunction and what remains for the courts to decide.

Client alerts should avoid turning “stay denied” into “policy finally struck down.” They should equally avoid minimizing the order’s present effect: interim relief can determine the conditions under which affected people receive care throughout lengthy proceedings.

The same discipline applies to statements about the justices. The order records two justices who would grant relief; it does not provide a reasoned account of every participating justice’s position.

The next stage remains important

Further developments should therefore be assessed against the lower-court reasons and the next stage of litigation.

The injunction continues to operate. The merits remain unresolved at the Supreme Court.
