Canada’s Equal-Treatment Wage Rules Are in Force: What Federal Employers Must Review

A different employment status is no longer a sufficient explanation for a lower wage rate where the new federal comparison requirements are met.

By The Legal CircleAnalysisOctober 21, 20262 min read

Analysis

Published by The Legal Circle

October 21, 2026

Canada (federally regulated workplaces)

A different employment status is no longer a sufficient explanation for a lower wage rate where the new federal comparison requirements are met.

Equal-treatment provisions under Part III of the Canada Labour Code took effect October 20. They restrict wage differences based on employment status in covered federally regulated workplaces. Labour Program: Equal Treatment interpretation.

When wage comparisons apply

The comparison requires employees to work in the same industrial establishment, perform substantially the same work requiring substantially the same skill, effort and responsibility, and work under similar conditions. The applicable type of wage rate must also be comparable. Labour Program: Equal Treatment interpretation.

The regime preserves qualifying systems based on seniority, merit, production and other prescribed criteria. The federal guidance emphasizes that an applicable system must cover comparable employees and be communicated in writing or readily available for examination. Labour Program: Equal Treatment interpretation.

For employers, the immediate issue is evidence. What explains a wage difference, and does the explanation match how compensation decisions actually operate?

A review should examine actual duties rather than relying solely on job titles. It should also test whether a claimed merit or seniority system is consistently applied.

Reviews, transition and temporary-help agencies

Employees can request a wage review, triggering a written response within 90 days. The amendments prohibit lowering wages to achieve compliance and provide protections against reprisals. Certain existing collective-agreement provisions receive a transition period of up to two years. Canada Gazette: final equal-treatment and temporary-help-agency regulations.

Related provisions address federally covered temporary-help agencies, including wage comparisons with client employees, prohibited fees and barriers to direct hiring. Canada Gazette: final equal-treatment and temporary-help-agency regulations.

The scope requires care. These are federal labour standards, not a general amendment to every Canadian employment relationship. The Gazette explanation also identifies the federal public service as outside Part III. Canada Gazette: final equal-treatment and temporary-help-agency regulations.

Test the payroll evidence

Counsel should prioritize compensation structures that distinguish between full-time, part-time, casual and temporary employees. The question is whether each difference has a permitted, supportable basis once the comparison criteria apply.

A written policy alone will not answer that question if the payroll record tells a different story.

The practical test is whether the employer can explain the wage difference with a system the evidence supports.
