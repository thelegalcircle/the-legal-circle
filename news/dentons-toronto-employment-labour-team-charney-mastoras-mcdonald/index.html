Dentons Adds 10-Lawyer Employment and Labour Team in Toronto

Adding an experienced partner expands a firm’s capabilities. Adding an established team can expand its capacity to deliver those capabilities immediately.

By The Legal CircleAnalysisOctober 14, 20263 min read
  • #Dentons Toronto employment team
Business & Practice Development

Analysis

Published by The Legal Circle

October 14, 2026

Ontario, Canada

Adding an experienced partner expands a firm’s capabilities. Adding an established team can expand its capacity to deliver those capabilities immediately.

Dentons announced on October 13 that a 10-lawyer employment and labour group led by Richard J. Charney, John Mastoras and Daniel McDonald had joined its Canadian practice in Toronto.

The firm highlighted experience in labour relations, employment and human rights litigation, workplace safety and executive employment, with particular strength serving unionized and federally regulated employers.

The move is a notable addition to the market for institutional workplace advice.

The practice mix explains the significance

Employer-side work can connect strategic advice with urgent proceedings.

A collective bargaining issue may develop into arbitration or labour-board litigation. A restructuring can raise employment, workplace communication and operational questions at the same time.

Dentons identifies Charney’s experience advising boards and management on sensitive workforce matters, while Mastoras’s practice includes labour arbitration, labour-board litigation and collective bargaining at provincial and federal levels.

Those capabilities help explain the commercial logic of a coordinated team addition.

The announcement establishes the recruitment. It does not, by itself, establish which clients or mandates will follow.

Why team moves differ from individual hires

A group can bring established working relationships, complementary experience and familiarity with how complex files are staffed.

That may reduce some of the coordination required when a firm builds a practice through separate hires over time.

Integration still requires work. Matter allocation, supervision, conflicts, systems and collaboration with existing lawyers all influence whether the added capacity translates into a consistent client experience.

For firm leaders, recruitment is therefore the beginning of the business case rather than its completion.

What institutional clients should assess

Clients reviewing an expanded practice should look beyond the number of lawyers.

Relevant questions include who will lead their matters, how the team will coordinate across jurisdictions and whether staffing arrangements support continuity during urgent disputes.

Clients should also understand how employment and labour advice connects with other needs, such as pensions, transactions or regulatory matters.

A larger platform can offer those connections. Their practical value depends on how work is organized.

What competitors can learn

For other firms, the move illustrates how quickly a practice can add visible depth through coordinated recruitment.

The appropriate response is not necessarily another large hire. Firms can assess their own client concentration, succession arrangements and ability to retain lawyers whose relationships anchor important work.

They should also distinguish recruitment announcements from evidence of changed market share. The latter requires time and information about client decisions.

Dentons has added a substantial group. How that group is integrated - and how clients respond - will determine the longer-term effect.

A team move adds people on day one. Its lasting value emerges in how they work together.
