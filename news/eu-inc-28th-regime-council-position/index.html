EU Inc Proposal Advances: What the Council’s “28th Regime” Position Would Change

Europe’s proposed optional corporate framework has reached another legislative stage, but founders cannot yet incorporate under it.

By The Legal CircleAnalysisOctober 24, 20262 min read
  • #EU Inc proposal
Legal Developments

Analysis

Published by The Legal Circle

October 24, 2026

European Union; Canadian corporate implications

Europe’s proposed optional corporate framework has reached another legislative stage, but founders cannot yet incorporate under it.

The Council of the European Union adopted its negotiating position on “EU Inc” on October 23. The proposal would offer an additional corporate framework alongside the 27 national regimes.

The Council describes online registration within two days at a maximum cost of €100, together with digital corporate processes. These are proposed features, not an incorporation service currently available under enacted EU Inc legislation. Council of the EU: negotiating position.

A negotiating position, not a live service

The position also addresses safeguards, employee participation and an employee stock-option framework. The size thresholds discussed in the Council announcement appear in the stock-option provisions; they should not be reported as a blanket eligibility limit for the entire corporate form. Council of the EU: negotiating position.

For corporate counsel, the proposal’s value will depend on how its common rules interact with national law.

Fast registration is useful, but it is only the beginning of a company’s legal life. Governance, financing, employee rights and transactions determine whether the framework simplifies cross-border operations over time.

National law still matters

The Council’s position preserves a role for national employee-participation rules and permits registration refusals on specified financial-crime grounds. Council of the EU: negotiating position.

That means the practical analysis cannot stop at the promise of one additional corporate label.

Canadian founders and investors considering European expansion should monitor the negotiations without building a transaction timetable around the proposal’s advertised registration period.

The final text will determine availability

The final text, approval process and applicable implementation arrangements will determine what becomes available and when.

For now, the Council has established its negotiating position. That is a significant step toward legislation, rather than the completion of it.

The test will be whether a common corporate option simplifies the company’s operations after registration as well as at formation.
