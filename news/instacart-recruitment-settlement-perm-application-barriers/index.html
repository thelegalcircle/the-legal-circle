Instacart Recruitment Settlement Shows How Application Barriers Create Legal Risk

Advertising a vacancy does not necessarily give every applicant a comparable opportunity to pursue it.

By The Legal CircleAnalysisOctober 21, 20262 min read
  • #Instacart recruitment settlement
Legal Developments

Analysis

Published by The Legal Circle

October 21, 2026

United States; Canadian comparison

Advertising a vacancy does not necessarily give every applicant a comparable opportunity to pursue it.

Instacart has agreed to a US$1 million civil penalty and a compensation fund of up to US$4 million to resolve federal allegations concerning recruitment associated with permanent labour certification.

The US Department of Justice and Equal Employment Opportunity Commission announced the settlement on October 20. DOJ and EEOC: Instacart settlement announcement.

How application methods matter

The agencies alleged that Instacart placed additional burdens on US applicants by requiring mailed applications for certain PERM positions, departing from its usual electronic application process. They said the difference affected timely consideration of applicants. DOJ and EEOC: Instacart settlement announcement.

Instacart maintains that its practices complied with applicable law. The settlement resolves the matter without an adjudicated liability finding. DOJ and EEOC: Instacart settlement announcement.

The agreement requires public career-site postings, electronic applications, revised policies, training, monitoring and reporting. DOJ and EEOC: Instacart settlement announcement.

Follow the applicant’s path

For employment and immigration counsel, the case illustrates why recruitment review must cover the entire application process.

The wording of an advertisement is only one component. Application methods, response times, screening practices and internal routing can determine whether an apparently open opportunity is accessible in practice.

A useful audit follows the applicant’s path. Where does the person find the role? How do they apply? Who receives the application? How is it assessed, and what record explains the outcome?

Comparing that process with the employer’s ordinary recruitment workflow may reveal differences that require explanation.

A US process with broader operational lessons

The PERM context is specific to US immigration law. Canadian employers should assess their obligations under the applicable domestic regimes rather than treating the agreement as a Canadian rule.

Its operational lesson travels more readily: compliance depends on how a recruitment system functions, as well as what its policy says.

Organizations should also ensure that immigration sponsorship and recruitment teams understand each other’s procedures. A formal process can create avoidable exposure when its practical requirements receive insufficient scrutiny.

An opportunity is only as accessible as the process used to apply for it.
