Paris Court Keeps Meta’s Interim Press-Rights Obligations in Force

Meta must continue complying with interim measures governing negotiations over French publishers’ content after losing its challenges in the Paris Court of Appeal.

Analysis

Published by The Legal Circle

October 29, 2026

France; Canadian platform-law comparison

Meta must continue complying with interim measures governing negotiations over French publishers’ content after losing its challenges in the Paris Court of Appeal.

The October 29 rulings leave measures imposed by France’s competition authority in July in place. They concern negotiations, information disclosure and the presentation of covered press content.

The rulings do not establish a final competition-law infringement.

What Meta must continue doing

The measures require negotiations with press organizations, disclosure of information concerning platform revenues and protected content, and restrictions on degrading how press content is displayed.

The dispute concerns publishers’ neighbouring rights: rights relating to the digital use of their publications.

Reuters reports that the court considered Meta’s practices capable of amounting to abuse of dominance. That preliminary assessment supports interim intervention; it is distinct from the eventual merits determination.

Meta’s response

Meta says it is assessing its next steps while continuing to comply and negotiate in good faith. No further appeal was confirmed in the sources reviewed.

Why competition lawyers should care

A right to negotiate payment can be difficult to exercise if one side controls information needed to assess the content’s use and value.

Interim measures can address that imbalance before the underlying investigation ends. Their significance lies partly in shaping the process: what information must be available, how parties engage and what conduct is restricted while negotiations continue.

For Canadian platform-regulation and media lawyers, the case offers a comparison rather than a direct statement of Canadian law. It illustrates how intellectual-property rights and competition remedies can interact when a dominant platform negotiates with content providers.

The final infringement question remains open. The immediate outcome is that the existing negotiating obligations continue.

Sources: Reuters report · French Competition Authority’s July order, Decision 26-MC-01
