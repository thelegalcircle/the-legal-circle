Apple Challenges US$5.7 Billion Patent Verdict Over iPhone Haptics

A jury’s verdict can produce a headline-making number. Whether that number survives judicial scrutiny is a separate question.

By The Legal CircleAnalysisOctober 14, 20262 min read
  • #Apple Taction patent verdict
Legal Developments

Analysis

Published by The Legal Circle

October 14, 2026

United States; Canadian implications

A jury’s verdict can produce a headline-making number. Whether that number survives judicial scrutiny is a separate question.

Apple has asked a U.S. federal judge in San Diego to overturn the US$5.7-billion patent verdict that Taction Technology won in September over haptic feedback technology used in iPhones and Apple Watches. Reuters described the original award as the largest U.S. patent verdict to date. Apple’s request remains unresolved.

For technology litigators, the dispute illustrates why the post-trial phase deserves as much attention as the jury’s decision.

Liability and valuation require separate analysis

Patent litigation asks more than whether a product infringes a protected invention. It also requires a legally supportable assessment of the compensation owed.

Those questions are connected, but they are not interchangeable. Establishing infringement does not, by itself, establish the monetary value of the technology.

For a multifunctional consumer device, valuation requires particular care. A phone or watch combines numerous technologies, features and commercial advantages. A damages analysis must explain how its proposed figure relates to the infringement at issue.

That is why counsel evaluating a major verdict need to examine the evidentiary route to the award, rather than treat its size as either proof of strength or proof of excess.

A post-trial request is not an appellate decision

The current development is Apple’s request to the trial court. It should not be described as a successful appeal or a judicial reduction of the award.

The next material development will be the judge’s response. Any subsequent appellate proceedings would involve a further procedural stage.

For clients following litigation exposure, those distinctions affect how outcomes are communicated. “The jury awarded damages,” “the court upheld the award” and “the appellate court affirmed” describe different events.

What Canadian counsel should take from the case

This is U.S. patent litigation. It does not establish Canadian damages law.

Its practical relevance is the importance of building a damages record that can withstand scrutiny after trial. Counsel should be able to connect the valuation method, assumptions and supporting evidence in a coherent explanation.

The same discipline matters when advising boards or commercial teams about litigation risk. A large verdict is significant, but its procedural status must remain visible in any assessment.

For now, Taction has obtained a major jury award, and Apple is challenging it. The court has yet to decide what follows.

A damages figure makes the news. The evidence supporting it determines its durability.
