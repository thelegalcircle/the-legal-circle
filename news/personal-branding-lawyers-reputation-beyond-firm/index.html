Personal Branding for Lawyers: Build a Reputation Beyond Your Firm’s Name

Does the market understand who you are professionally, or does it only know where you work?

  • #Professional Visibility
  • #Law Firm Marketing

Analysis

Written by Adele Salikhova

October 6, 2026

Canada

She was the best-kept secret in the law firm.

That was how I opened a LinkedIn post about a lawyer whose clients followed her when she moved firms. The experience made me think about where professional trust actually sits.

A firm’s name can attract attention. Its resources, reputation and team can help a client decide to make contact. But once the relationship develops, the client also experiences a particular lawyer: how she explains a problem, responds to a question and handles a difficult conversation.

Over time, those experiences can become associated with her name.

The story raises a useful question for lawyers and firm leaders alike: does the market understand who you are professionally, or does it only know where you work?

Your employer and your reputation are connected

A law firm provides important foundations for a lawyer’s work. Colleagues contribute expertise. Support staff help deliver service. Established systems, institutional relationships and the firm’s reputation all matter.

An individual reputation develops within that environment.

Clients and referral partners may come to associate a lawyer with a particular area of judgment, a way of communicating or an ability to manage difficult matters. Those associations give substance to a personal brand.

The strongest version reflects work that others can recognize.

A lawyer who regularly explains complex commercial issues clearly may become known for that ability. A lawyer who contributes useful analysis to professional discussions may become a person colleagues remember when a related question arises.

Visibility helps people place your expertise

Excellent work does not automatically communicate itself beyond the people directly involved.

A referral partner may know that you are a good lawyer while remaining uncertain about the matters you want to handle. A professional contact may remember your firm but struggle to describe your contribution.

Useful visibility closes that gap.

A clear biography explains your focus. A thoughtful article demonstrates how you approach a question. A presentation helps a relevant audience understand your experience.

These activities make professional reputation more legible.

The goal is for someone to describe your work accurately: who you help, what you handle and why a conversation with you may be worthwhile.

A personal brand should reflect the real lawyer

Visibility creates expectations.

If your articles are practical and accessible, people may expect clear explanations in a consultation. If your profile emphasizes responsiveness, they will notice how you handle an introduction.

The public message and the professional experience need to fit together.

That does not require adopting a polished online personality. A measured lawyer can write measured commentary. A specialist can publish narrowly focused analysis. Someone who prefers teaching to self-promotion can build recognition through useful explanations.

Consistency is easier to maintain when the communication grows from your actual work and interests.

Five ways to build a credible personal brand

1. Define a professional focus

Write a sentence explaining whom you help and the kinds of matters you handle.

Make it specific enough to guide a referral. Avoid relying entirely on broad descriptions such as “trusted adviser” or “results-oriented counsel.”

Use the same core explanation across your biography and professional profiles.

2. Publish within your experience

Choose questions you encounter regularly and can explain responsibly.

Discuss a recurring issue, a practical distinction or a development within your field. Protect confidential information and keep qualifications visible where they matter.

A useful contribution gives readers a reason to remember your judgment.

3. Develop relationships outside your immediate team

Participate in professional associations, educational events and relevant conversations.

Build connections with people whose work intersects with yours. Make room for listening and collaboration.

A professional network becomes more valuable when people understand one another’s capabilities.

4. Keep your public information current

Review your firm biography, LinkedIn profile, speaking descriptions and other professional listings.

Check that they accurately describe your role, experience and contact information. Remove outdated claims or services you no longer provide.

An interested reader should be able to establish what you do now.

5. Support recognition across the firm

For firm leaders, make individual expertise visible alongside the institution’s capabilities.

Give lawyers opportunities to contribute, speak and develop relationships. Explain how teams collaborate so clients can recognize both the responsible lawyer and the support behind the work.

Individual recognition can strengthen the firm’s overall reputation.

Career changes reveal where recognition has developed

A lawyer’s move can bring professional reputation into sharper view. Clients make their own choices, and transitions involve responsibilities that require careful handling.

The marketing lesson does not depend on predicting who will follow whom.

It is that a career benefits from a reputation people can associate with your own name. The firm matters. Your contribution should also be understandable.

The lawyer in my original post had become known through the experience of working with her. Her name meant something specific to the people who trusted her.

Make your expertise visible enough that people can remember it by name.
