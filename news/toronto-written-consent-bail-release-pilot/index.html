Toronto’s Written Bail Release Pilot Begins - with Judicial Approval Still Required

When the Crown and defence agree on a straightforward bail release, the remaining process can still consume courtroom time.

By The Legal CircleAnalysisOctober 19, 20262 min read
  • #Toronto written consent release pilot
Legal Developments

Analysis

Published by The Legal Circle

October 19, 2026

Toronto, Ontario, Canada

When the Crown and defence agree on a straightforward bail release, the remaining process can still consume courtroom time.

A Toronto pilot beginning October 19 aims to streamline that stage through written consent-release applications. Announced by the Ontario Court of Justice on October 15, the Toronto implementation initially applies only to duty counsel matters. Ontario Court of Justice: Toronto pilot announcement.

The process permits a judicial officer to consider an agreed release in writing outside court. It does not make Crown consent sufficient for release.

Consent does not replace judicial approval

Under the governing practice direction, defence counsel must review the proposed conditions with the accused and confirm that the accused understands and consents to written consideration in their absence. The judicial officer may approve or refuse the proposed release. Written Consent Release Pilot Project Practice Direction.

There is also a further safeguard: before the order is made, the accused must appear before the issuing judicial officer by audio, video or in person to acknowledge and enter it on the record. Written Consent Release Pilot Project Practice Direction.

That makes the pilot a change in how agreed releases are processed, rather than an automatic-release mechanism.

Measuring the pilot

Its practical promise is straightforward. If suitable matters require less courtroom and videoconferencing capacity, other cases may receive attention sooner. Whether that promise is realized will require evidence from implementation.

Useful measures would include the time between agreement and release, the frequency of incomplete applications, how often judicial officers refer matters back to court and whether accused people understand their conditions.

Clear materials and client understanding

For counsel, the immediate work remains substantive. Agreement should follow a careful review of the proposed terms and the client’s circumstances. Moving consideration into a written process increases the importance of preparing clear, complete materials.

The pilot also deserves evaluation from the accused person’s perspective. A faster administrative pathway is valuable when it produces a timely, understandable order. Speed alone will not establish that the process works well.

Toronto’s implementation offers a focused opportunity to improve an agreed stage of bail proceedings while retaining a judicial decision.

The measure of success will be how quickly - and clearly - an appropriate release reaches the person waiting for it.
