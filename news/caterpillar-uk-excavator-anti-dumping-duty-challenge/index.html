Caterpillar Challenges UK Anti-Dumping Duty on Chinese-Made Excavators

How should a regulator calculate a product’s value when it believes the price of key inputs has been distorted?

By The Legal CircleAnalysisOctober 27, 20262 min read
  • #Caterpillar UK excavator anti-dumping duty
Legal Developments

Analysis

Published by The Legal Circle

October 27, 2026

United Kingdom; Canadian trade-law comparison

How should a regulator calculate a product’s value when it believes the price of key inputs has been distorted?

That question sits at the centre of Caterpillar’s challenge to a UK anti-dumping duty on certain excavators made in China.

A Caterpillar subsidiary is contesting the 18.81% duty, arguing that the UK Trade Remedies Authority’s calculation inflated its costs and profits. The authority defends its approach to alleged market distortions. The matter was heard in London; no new merits ruling was identified in the reporting reviewed.

The calculation dispute

Anti-dumping duties can follow a finding that imported goods are being sold at unfairly low prices under the applicable trade-remedy rules.

Here, Caterpillar disputes how the authority calculated the relevant costs and profits. The regulator’s position is that distorted input prices, including for materials such as steel, justify its method, according to the Financial Times.

The parties therefore disagree not merely about a percentage, but about the assumptions used to arrive at it.

How this challenge differs from the earlier case

The current dispute concerns the final duty calculation. Caterpillar previously brought an unsuccessful procedural challenge concerning notice of the investigation.

That earlier result does not decide the merits of this calculation challenge. Nor does the hearing establish that the tariff will be changed.

For trade lawyers, the case is a significant test of how the UK’s post-Brexit trade-remedy system handles allegations that input prices do not reflect ordinary market conditions.

For Canadian businesses operating across borders, it also illustrates why a trade measure must be examined at the level of methodology: an adjustment to a cost assumption can have a direct effect on the duty applied to an import.

Sources: Financial Times report · Law360 hearing report
