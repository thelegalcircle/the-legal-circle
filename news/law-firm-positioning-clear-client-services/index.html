Law Firm Positioning: Make Your Services Easier for Clients to Understand

A client should not need legal training to understand what your firm offers. Five ways to clarify services, scope and the first step.

Analysis

Written by Adele Salikhova

October 6, 2026

Canada

A family law firm was the subject of an earlier LinkedIn post I wrote about positioning. The idea was simple: a practice becomes easier to understand when it presents a few clear services instead of an undifferentiated list of everything it can do.

That distinction matters because clients arrive with situations.

They may need advice before signing a separation agreement. They may be uncertain about parenting arrangements. They may have received documents and need to understand what happens next.

A website that lists divorce, custody, support, property division and litigation identifies the field. It may still leave the visitor unsure where to begin.

Clear law firm positioning helps someone connect their circumstances to an appropriate first step.

A practice-area list leaves interpretation to the client

Lawyers understand the relationships between services. Prospective clients often do not.

A person navigating a separation may struggle to distinguish negotiation, mediation and litigation. They may not know whether they need ongoing representation or advice on a specific document.

When the website presents every service with equal emphasis, it asks that person to classify their own problem before contacting the firm.

Some will call anyway. Others may continue searching for a practice that appears to understand their situation more directly.

The communication opportunity is to explain whom the firm helps, what assistance is available and how an inquiry progresses.

That explanation can be precise without attempting to assess the visitor’s legal position.

Focus is a communication decision

A firm does not need to abandon its capabilities to make its message clearer.

It can lead with the work it most wants to attract while explaining the broader scope elsewhere. It can organize services around recognizable client situations. It can distinguish an initial consultation from an ongoing engagement.

Consider the difference between:

“Comprehensive family law solutions.”

And:

“Advice for people reviewing a proposed separation agreement.”

The second gives the reader a situation they can recognize. It also creates room to explain what the service involves, how suitability is assessed and what the client should prepare.

Specificity supports informed inquiry. It does not require a promise about the outcome.

Clear services need operational support

A well-written service page can create expectations that the practice must be able to meet.

If the firm describes a document-review service, its team should understand the proposed scope, how inquiries are screened and how terms are confirmed.

If the homepage invites people facing urgent issues to contact the firm, someone needs responsibility for assessing those inquiries appropriately.

Positioning therefore belongs in conversations about both marketing and delivery.

A service description that sounds attractive but leaves staff guessing is unfinished. The external explanation should reflect an internal process the team can follow.

Five ways to clarify your firm’s offering

1. Choose a recognizable client situation

Start with a situation your firm regularly handles and wants to attract.

Describe it in the language clients use, then connect it to the relevant legal service. “Reviewing an employment contract” is easier to recognize than a broad reference to workplace advisory work.

Keep the description accurate to your capabilities.

2. Explain what the engagement may involve

Give prospective clients a practical overview.

What is the purpose of the initial conversation? What information might the lawyer need? How are scope and fees established?

Explain the process without implying that every matter follows the same path.

3. Separate the first step from the full matter

A prospective client may hesitate because they cannot picture the commitment involved.

Make the initial step clear. Explain whether it is an intake call, a consultation or another defined process, and identify any relevant charge.

That clarity helps people decide whether to proceed.

4. Give each important service its own explanation

A focused service page can address the questions associated with a particular need.

Use a clear heading, explain whom the service is intended for and show how to contact the firm. Avoid repeating generic claims across every page.

The reader should learn something useful before reaching the contact button.

5. Review the inquiries you receive

Ask intake staff which questions arise repeatedly and where prospective clients misunderstand the offering.

Use that information to refine the website. If many people contact the firm for work it does not handle, examine whether the message is too broad.

Clarity improves through observation.

A clearer message makes expertise easier to find

Broad language can feel reassuring inside a firm because it leaves every possibility open. For a prospective client, it may leave the central question unanswered: can this practice help with my situation?

The family law example behind my original post offers a useful principle for any practice. Make the service understandable before asking someone to take the next step.

A client should not need legal training to understand what your firm offers.
