Milbank Expands Defence Work as Law Firms Face Civil RICO Claims

Milbank is developing a defence niche as personal-injury firms face civil RICO allegations. What Canadian lawyers can learn without equating legal systems.

By The Legal CircleAnalysisOctober 10, 20264 min read
  • #law firm RICO claims
Business & Practice Development

Analysis

Published by The Legal Circle

October 10, 2026

United States

A dispute over injury claims can become a lawsuit against the lawyers who brought them.

Milbank has taken at least its third engagement defending personal-injury lawyers against racketeering allegations. Its latest client, Asher & Associates, faces a New York City lawsuit alleging fabricated roadway-injury claims. Defence counsel disputes that characterization and says the case improperly recasts contested litigation as racketeering.

The allegations remain unproven. The broader development is the emergence of a defence practice focused on claims against law firms themselves.

The allegations move beyond the individual file

Ordinary litigation tests the facts and legal entitlement in a particular claim.

An enterprise-level allegation asks different questions about repeated conduct, relationships and the way claims are generated or supported.

That change can expand the focus from the client’s case to the firm’s own operations.

The distinction is important for reporting. A recurring allegation is not proof of a coordinated scheme. Nor does retaining specialist defence counsel establish liability.

Two competing accounts require careful treatment

A claimant may argue that litigation activity forms part of an unlawful operation.

A defendant firm may respond that the allegations weaponize litigation against lawyers representing injured people.

Those positions cannot be resolved through rhetoric alone. They require examination of the pleaded elements, evidence and procedural decisions.

Coverage should preserve the difference between challenging a case and proving misconduct by the lawyer who advanced it.

The Canadian relevance is comparative

U.S. civil RICO litigation does not create a corresponding Canadian cause of action merely because similar allegations could arise here.

Canadian counsel would need to analyze domestic law separately.

The practical value of the story is as a risk signal: disputes may scrutinize intake practices, evidence handling, third-party relationships and the documentation of professional decisions.

A firm’s records can become important when an allegation concerns how its work was conducted across multiple matters.

Governance should support defensible decisions

For law firm leaders, a useful review focuses on how concerns are identified and escalated.

Who evaluates questionable source material? How are inconsistencies in client instructions addressed? What checks apply to relationships with third parties? How are significant decisions documented?

These questions do not suggest that the defendant firms lacked particular controls. They identify operational issues worth examining when litigation targets professional conduct.

A well-designed process should help lawyers respond to genuine concerns without treating every contested claim as evidence of fraud.

A legal niche follows a changing risk

Milbank’s engagements illustrate how a recurring litigation theory can generate specialized demand.

The defence task combines knowledge of the underlying litigation with the ability to challenge allegations about a firm’s broader activities.

For Canadian readers, the immediate lesson is to watch the development without overstating its reach. The existence of lawsuits demonstrates conflict, not the truth of the claims or an inevitable trend northward.

When the firm becomes the defendant, how it practised can become part of the case.
